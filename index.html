Which curve shifts — and who gets each area?

The first three diagrams show one importing market. In each of them the same four areas tile the band between the world price line and the new price line — no gaps, no overlaps. What changes is who ends up with area 3. The fourth applies the same demand and supply curves to an exporting country, where the world price sits above the autarky price — there the four areas are labelled a–d.

Same areas, different destinations

Figures update live with the sliders. Every number here is a size, never a signed figure — the word beside it says which way it goes: gains / loses for households and firms, collects / pays for the government. Everything is measured as a change from free trade.

Every shaded polygon is measured with the shoelace formula and checked against a point-in-polygon sweep, so the four areas are guaranteed to tile the band exactly. In the export subsidy panel the same curves face a world price of PW = 22, above autarky — the country exports Q3 − Q2 = 10 units in free trade — and there the four areas a–d tile the rectangle from Pw to Pw+s.
